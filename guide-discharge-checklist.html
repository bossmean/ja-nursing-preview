The Hospital Discharge Checklist

Discharge usually happens faster than families expect, often on a day nobody planned for. This is the list to work through before the car pulls up, built from the gaps Michelle has watched send people straight back to emergency.

Back to the free tools

How to use this

Print it and take it to the hospital. Work down the list with the nurse or discharge planner and tick items off as you get real answers, not assumed ones. If an item cannot be answered before discharge, write down who will answer it and when.

Ask for the written discharge summary before you leave the building. Verbal instructions do not survive the drive home.

1. Medications

  • The full list on discharge. Every medication, the dose, and how often. Get it in writing.
  • What was stopped or changed, and why. This is where most errors happen. Compare the discharge list against what was being taken at home, item by item.
  • Who fills the new prescriptions, and when. Confirm the pharmacy has them and whether anything needs to be started today.
  • Anything to avoid. Foods, alcohol, driving, over the counter products and existing supplements.

2. Warning signs

  • Which symptoms mean call the office or the unit. Get the number and the hours.
  • Which symptoms mean go to emergency or call 911. Ask for this explicitly. Do not infer it.
  • What is normal for this recovery. Knowing what is expected prevents both panic and dangerous waiting.
  • Who to call after hours. Nights and weekends are when families are most often stranded.

3. Home and safety

  • Can they get in the door. Stairs, thresholds, and whether the usual entrance still works.
  • Bathroom, bed and chair. Height, grab bars, and whether transfers are safe with the help available at home.
  • Equipment needed. Walker, commode, raised seat, shower chair, oxygen. Confirm what is arranged versus what the family has to source.
  • Trip hazards cleared. Rugs, cords, clutter on the path between bed, bathroom and kitchen.

4. Care and follow up

  • What care is arranged at home. Home care nursing, personal support, physiotherapy. Who arranged it, and when does the first visit happen.
  • Wound, drain, catheter or dressing care. Who does it, how often, and has someone at home been shown how.
  • Diet, activity and lifting limits. Including how long each restriction lasts.
  • Follow up appointments. Booked or not booked, with whom, and whose job it is to book them.

5. Paperwork and people

  • The written discharge summary in hand. Ask for a copy before you leave.
  • The family doctor knows. Confirm the summary is being sent, and take a copy to the next appointment in case it is not.
