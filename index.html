Does this sound familiar

The system is not built to slow down for you.

Appointments are short. Language is technical. The important questions arrive at two in the morning, long after the office has closed. None of that means you are doing something wrong.

You left the appointment and realized you still do not understand what happens next.
You are managing an aging parent's care from a group chat and a shoebox of paperwork.
Your short term disability claim was denied and the letter did not really say why.
A loved one came home from hospital and nobody explained the recovery plan.
You have four specialists and none of them seem to be talking to each other.
You freeze in the room, then remember every question in the car park.
People are not lacking services. They are lacking understanding. That single realization, after Michelle lost her sister to cancer in 2002, became the foundation of this work.

How Michelle helps

Five places where the right support changes the outcome.

The goal is never to make decisions for you. It is to make sure you understand your options well enough to make them yourself.

Health care navigation

Understand a diagnosis, prepare for appointments, decode tests and referrals, organize medications, and walk in with the right questions already written down.

Caregiver and family support

Supporting an aging parent, sharing the load across siblings, understanding community resources, and having the power of attorney and future care conversation before a crisis forces it.

Post surgery and recovery

Discharge plan review, home safety, pain management check ins, and early identification of the complications that send people back to emergency.

Disability and workplace

Short and long term disability, workplace accommodation, return to work planning and workers' compensation concerns, from a Certified Return to Work Coordinator who has been on both sides of the claim.

Health and social advocacy

Mental health navigation, housing and community supports, and the confidence to advocate for yourself once the sessions end.

Michelle Smith, RPN, CRTWC

She has been the nurse. She has also been the patient.

In 2017, while working as a nurse at one of Toronto's largest hospitals, Michelle became ill and found herself navigating the disability system from the other side. Even with the training and the vocabulary, she was buried in paperwork, financial uncertainty and a process nobody explained.

The question that came out of it is the reason this practice exists. If this can happen to a health care professional, what is happening to everyone else?

  • Registered Practical Nurse since 2002, built on RN level education and clinical training
  • Certified Return to Work Coordinator through NIDMAR and Pacific Coast University
  • Founder of JA Smith Consulting and Nursing Services, 2011, named for her children Jessica and Anthony
  • Founder of JA Nursing We Care Inc., 2015
  • Contributor to The Toronto Caribbean Newspaper, and a health care segment on 105.5 FM
