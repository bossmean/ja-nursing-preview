The 7 Questions Kit

Most appointments are shorter than the problem deserves. These seven questions are the ones Michelle asks clients to write down before they walk in, because they are the ones that decide what actually happens next.

Back to the free tools

How to use this

Print the sheet at the end, or copy the seven questions into your phone. Fill in what you can before the appointment. Take it with you, and give a second copy to whoever comes with you so somebody is writing while you are listening.

You will not get to all seven every time. Ask them in order and stop when the appointment ends. The order is deliberate.

The seven questions

  1. What is the name of this, in plain words, and how sure are you?

    Ask for the actual diagnosis or the working diagnosis, and whether it is confirmed or suspected. Those are two different situations and they lead to different next steps. Write down the exact word so you can spell it later.

  2. What are my options, including doing nothing for now?

    There is almost always more than one path. Asking for the full set, including watchful waiting where it applies, turns a recommendation into a decision you are part of.

  3. What happens if we wait, and what would make you want to move faster?

    This is the question that tells you how urgent the situation really is, and gives you the specific signs that should change the plan.

  4. What should I watch for at home, and at what point do I call versus go to emergency?

    Ask for the dividing line explicitly. Knowing which symptoms mean phone the office and which mean go now prevents both a dangerous wait and an unnecessary night in a waiting room.

  5. How will this interact with everything else I take?

    Bring the list, including over the counter products, vitamins and anything herbal. This is where the medication log earns its place.

  6. Who is responsible for the next step, and when should I expect it?

    Referrals, test bookings and results all move faster when somebody has said out loud whose job it is and roughly how long it takes. Write down the name and the timeframe.

  7. If I have not heard anything by that date, who do I call?

    The most commonly skipped question, and the one that catches the referral that quietly never got sent. Get a name and a number before you leave the room.

Before you leave the room

Say this out loud: so what I am hearing is, and then repeat the plan back in your own words. If you cannot repeat it, you do not have it yet, and thirty seconds now saves a week of uncertainty. It also gives your provider a chance to correct anything you misheard.

Your one page preparation sheet

Fill this in before the appointment.

Appointment preparation sheet

Date, time and who I am seeing
The main reason I am here, in one sentence
What has changed since the last visit
My top three questions, in order of importance
Medications, supplements and doses I take
The answer to question 6: who does the next step and by when
The answer to question 7: who I call if nothing happens
What I will say back before I leave: so what I am hearing is
