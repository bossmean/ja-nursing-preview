Health care advocacy

Health care is complicated. Understanding your options should not be.

A new diagnosis. An aging parent. A denied claim. A recovery that is not going the way it was supposed to. Whatever brought you here, you can bring it to a free consultation and leave with a clearer picture of what to do next.

What is a health care advocate

Someone whose only job in the room is you.

A health care advocate helps individuals and families understand, navigate and organize their health care journey. The goal is not to make decisions for you. It is to help you understand your options, ask informed questions, reach the right resources, and make decisions with more confidence.

Michelle works as a Registered Practical Nurse, a Certified Return to Work Coordinator and a social advocate, which means both sides of a health problem get attention. Health challenges rarely happen in isolation. Income, housing, work, family and culture all shape how a diagnosis actually plays out.

Advocacy support is educational and organizational. It does not replace medical advice, diagnosis or treatment from your own providers, and it is not an emergency service.

How Michelle helps

Five areas of support

Health care navigation

  • Understand diagnoses and treatment recommendations
  • Prepare for health care appointments
  • Navigate specialist referrals
  • Understand tests, results and recommendations
  • Organize medications and health information
  • Prepare questions for health care providers

Caregiver and family support

  • Support aging parents
  • Navigate caregiving responsibilities
  • Understand community resources
  • Power of attorney and future care planning discussions
  • Family health care advocacy

Post surgery and recovery support

  • Discharge planning and home safety review
  • Pain management and physiotherapy compliance check ins
  • Early identification of post surgical complications
  • Support when a loved one is recovering but struggling to follow the care plan
  • Coordination of follow up care and recovery resources
  • Family guidance and advocacy through recovery

Disability and workplace support

  • Short term disability
  • Long term disability
  • Workplace accommodations
  • Return to work planning
  • Workers' compensation concerns

Health and social advocacy

  • Mental health navigation
  • Community resource referrals
  • Housing and social support connections
  • Understanding available services and supports
  • Building confidence as a self advocate

Why advocacy makes a difference

Throughout her nursing career Michelle has watched how hard health care decisions can be. What surprised her most was not that people lacked intelligence or motivation. It was that they were expected to navigate a complex system without the knowledge, support or confidence to advocate for themselves.

Some of the most important decisions happen between appointments. Questions arrive after you leave the office. Treatment choices need to be made. Family members need support. Results need explaining. New diagnoses create uncertainty.

Sometimes what people need most is not more information. It is someone to help them make sense of it.
